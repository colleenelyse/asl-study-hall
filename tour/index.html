ASL Study: the app your student uses in ASL class

Your student’s ASL teacher built this app for their classes: practice with real sign videos, a 3D hand that fingerspells and signs numbers, a webcam that reads your student’s fingerspelling, story games, class games for the projector, and tools that help the teacher see who needs help. 1 · Practice every sign

Real sign videos, many ways to practice

Every vocabulary sign from class has a short video. Students pick the units they are studying, and the app keeps track of which signs they know, which are new, and which keep tripping them up.

Practice

Learn, review, and remember

  • Learn: a few signs at a time until they stick.
  • Daily Review: the signs your student is about to forget come back.
  • Type It and Say It: see the sign, give the meaning.
  • Streaks, a test countdown, and a Dictionary of every sign.

2 · Fingerspelling and numbers

A 3D hand that signs, and a webcam that reads your student’s hand

Fingerspelling and ASL numbers are some of the hardest parts of the language. The app has a 3D hand that signs any word or number at any speed, and a webcam game where your student signs to the camera and the app checks each handshape.

Watch

Type anything: the hand fingerspells it

Words, names, whole sentences, from beginner speed up to the speed Deaf signers use, in different skin tones. Then games flip it around: the hand spells, your student types what they saw.

Try the 3D handAll the games
Numbers

Numbers the way they are really signed

ASL numbers follow their own rules: 2,026 is signed 2 · THOUSAND · 26, with two hands. The hand signs any number up to 999,999,999, plus years, ages, times, money and math games.

Try any numberTry a year
📷 Webcam

Fingerspell to the camera

Your student signs each letter or number to the webcam, and the app recognizes the handshape and moves on. It teaches itself your student’s own hand, with games, a speed pacer and a “show me the handshape” helper.

🔒 The camera only watches the hand while the game is open, on your own computer. No video or picture is saved or sent anywhere.
