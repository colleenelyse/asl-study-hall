For principals, special-education directors and district staff

A musical where every student has a part, and can learn it.

The Adaptive Theatre app runs a full Broadway Junior musical for a class of students with and without disabilities. It turns the licensed script into practice each student can do on their own, in the way that works for them.

1Why this app

Theatre tools assume every actor can read and practice alone.

Rehearsal apps expect actors to read a script, follow a printed schedule and practice by themselves. Accessibility tools know nothing about the show. So students with disabilities end up depending on an adult for every line, every cue and every entrance.

This app is built around the actual show and the actual student. The teacher imports the licensed script on her own device, and the app builds everything from it: every scene, every line by speaker, every song, who sings each part, and the stage directions.

🧑‍🎤Who it serves

Emerging readers, nonverbal students and AAC users, students who sign, autistic students, students with sensory needs, and students with vision or hearing needs, side by side with their peers.

📱Where it runs

School iPads and Chromebooks, the teacher's laptop and the projector. It installs like an app and opens without Wi-Fi once it's on the device.

🤝The Company

Company Artists (students with disabilities) and Company Mentors (peer leaders) rehearse, perform and run crews together.

🔁Repeats every semester

Built for any licensed Broadway Junior or KIDS script. The first show is in fall 2027.

2Student view · A student's day

Every student knows where to go and what "done" looks like.

When a student opens the app, Today shows their job for each half of class in large type, with a Start button that goes straight to the activity.

  • Two blocks a day. Each block is a teacher's room or a task card.
  • Every task card says what "done" looks like, so a student can finish without asking.
  • Weeks are shown one at a time, and every week follows the same pattern: Character Monday, Music Tuesday, Scene Wednesday, Dance Thursday, Full-Out Friday.
  • Every student has something to do every day, including a weekly crew job.
Why it matters: a visual schedule and predictable routines are evidence-based supports for autistic learners. They also make transitions easier for everyone. See the research.

3Student view · Try it

Practice Lines: the app reads the other parts and listens for yours.

You are Theo in our made-up sample show, The Bake Sale. The app plays Rosa's lines. When it's your turn, say your line, type it, or tap I did it.

  • Paraphrase is OK. It checks the words that carry the meaning, not every word in order, and it catches a dropped "not".
  • Loose, medium or exact checking, set for each student.
  • Hints that fade: first the line, then first letters, then hidden, and it steps back after help.
  • Many ways to answer: speak, sign, use AAC (the "I did it" button), or point and tap.
  • Each try is counted, so the teacher sees what needs work.
Why it matters: prompting with fading is an evidence-based practice. Trying to recall a line beats rereading it (retrieval practice). AAC is a recognized support, not an afterthought.

4Student view · The real script

The licensed pages, with each student's part lit up in their color.

Students see the real script pages, not a retyped copy. Their own lines and sung lines are highlighted in their character's color, and other characters' lines can be shown too.

  • Feeling pictures beside the lines, labeled with who they are for, so a student knows how a line should feel.
  • Director's notes (purple) everyone sees, and private notes only the student sees.
  • A printable highlighted script in the colors the student picks.
  • A plain-language story of every scene, with sensory warnings (loud music, flashing lights).

5Student view · Try it

My Songs: when to sing, and when to wait.

Each student's song page plays the show's rehearsal tracks (with or without the guide singers) and lights up the line being sung on the real sheet music, in the singer's color.

  • Red X mode: a red X covers the screen while other people sing, and a green "Sing!" frame shows on the student's own lines. It's for a student who sings every line and needs to learn when to wait.
  • A piano plays their own part in a harmony, read from the sheet music.
  • Loop a hard section, slow it down, play the whole show with the screen off, and watch the class's own dance videos.
  • After a song, students rate how well they know the words and the dance.

Pick who you are, then press play. This sample song has made-up words.

Why it matters: music-mediated intervention and visual supports are both on the national list of evidence-based practices for autistic learners. The red X is a visual cue that can fade as the student learns their part.

6Student view · Try it

React to That! Practice showing a feeling on cue.

An ensemble actor's biggest job is to react. React to That! reads an announcement aloud, says "React!", and then shows the feeling that fits, with a picture and how to show it with your face and body.

  • About 150 announcements in three levels: big feelings, quieter feelings, then reacting as your character.
  • Show it (react, then see) and Name it (pick the feeling from pictures).
  • Show Me shows the picture first, so practice never feels like a test.
  • An optional camera mirror plays the student's own face back beside the picture. It's never saved or sent.
  • The app never grades a face. The student decides "Yes / Some / Not yet."
Why it matters: modeling, visual supports and video self-modeling are evidence-based practices. Drama-based programs with peer actors have been shown to improve social skills for autistic youth.
Press Start. Listen, then react with your face and body.
7Student view · Make it fit

Each student sets the app up their way.

Text size, fonts made for readability (Atkinson Hyperlegible, Lexend, Andika, Comic Neue), letter and line spacing, light or dark, high contrast, a main color and page tint, less motion, symbols beside words, and their own read-aloud voice. Touch targets are big, and each screen does one thing.

Try it now: the controls change this whole tour.

Why it matters: this is Universal Design for Learning: the same content, more than one way to see it, hear it and respond. Fixing the screen for one student doesn't change it for anyone else.

🔊 Read aloud

Every line, announcement and story can be read aloud. Each student picks the voice for their own lines, and names are pronounced the teacher's way.

⌨️ Keyboard

On Chromebooks: Space plays, arrows move, H gives a hint, M turns on the microphone.

🖨️ Print

A highlighted script, a calendar for each role, and a call board, for students who work best on paper.

📴 Offline

A class iPad keeps the show, so practice keeps going when the Wi-Fi doesn't.

8Student view · Try it

The curtain call: a chime, then a big GO.

My bow tells each student their bow group, which side they walk on from, where they stand and who they bow with, and lets them practice with the bow music.

In rehearsal, the projector plays the music. Four seconds before each group, a soft chime sounds. Then a full-screen GO appears in the group's color. Students learn to walk on by themselves, without an adult's hand on their shoulder.

Why it matters: the chime is a warning before the change, and GO is a clear visual cue. Both are supports that fade: by the performance, students know the music.

9Teacher view · Planning

A full semester planned before the cast list exists.

The class list arrives about a week before school starts. The planner fills every class day from the school calendar, for every role, and rebuilds when casting is posted.

  • Two blocks a day, two teacher rooms plus Group C (task cards).
  • Checked every block: nobody in two places, nobody with nothing to do.
  • No student does the same kind of work three days in a row.
  • Hardest scenes first, with review days. Music before dance.
  • A weekly crew day for all but the leads. Big numbers include the whole ensemble.
  • School holidays, short days, theatre bookings and field trips are built in.
  • Two versions of every task card: with trained Mentors, or two teachers only.
Why it matters: every student is in the plan every day, with variety, so nobody sits and waits and nobody is forgotten for weeks.

🎙️ Mic plot

Works out which performer wears which body mic, scene by scene, with every swap written onto that student's schedule: when, where and who helps.

📋 Cue sheet

Suggested light, sound and music cues from the script, numbered the way the teacher works. Accept, edit, print.

🎤 Auditions and casting

Students sign up on the iPad: roles they want, comfort with lines, singing and dancing, and how they want to audition (in class, on video, with a buddy, with their talker or sign). Notes, callbacks and a cast list. Gender is never asked.

💬 Lines for AAC

Every spoken line for a character in show order, each with its cue, ready to copy into a student's AAC device, print, or save with the recorded voices.

🎧 Voices with feeling

Recorded character voices with a tone for each line (nervous, sarcastic, whispering), a little clearer than usual, for students who find tone of voice hard to read. Checks run before anything is made.

⏱️ School-day show

Plans a 45-minute version for in-school performances from the teacher's own ranked cuts, alongside the full evening show.

👕 Costumes and shopping

Costume plots, student costume research with tap-to-pick reasons, a shopping field trip with a "Found it!" list, photos of every finished look, and sizes with follow-ups.

🛠️ Crews and the call board

Home crews, crew days, attendance, days away, and a printable call board grouped by job.

🌟 Curtain call

Bow groups in order, timed to the bow music, with a projector mode for rehearsal and My bow for each student.

12Family view

A family portal for news, forms and practice at home.

  • Needs your answer: clothing and shoe sizes, sensory and clothing needs (fabrics, tags, makeup), the show-week commitment with the dates, permission to help with trying on clothes, and video permission.
  • Announcements pop up once, in large text that can be read aloud. A Notifications page keeps them all.
  • Email updates from the school account, with reminders before show dates. The teacher approves every reminder before it goes.
  • Practice at home: songs, the story, costumes and the calendar, only the pages the teacher opens to families.
  • Volunteer and donate, including a Company dinner where each family brings part of the meal.
  • Families with more than one Company member see a card for each child.

13Privacy and safety

Student information is locked before it leaves the device.

🔐 Encrypted

Names, photos, notes, the script text and the tracks are encrypted on the device. The server only stores scrambled data.

🔑 Separate passwords

One password opens the show for the cast; a separate teacher password is needed to change anything. Wrong guesses are limited.

🎟️ Each password opens only what's chosen

Students, Mentors and parents each see only the pages the teacher picks.

📧 No student email addresses kept

Students sign in with their school Google account. The app keeps a scrambled code to match the class list, never the address.

👪 Parents checked by Google

The server checks a parent's Google sign-in before family pages open. Contact details sit in the teacher's locked records.

🚫 No ads, no tracking

The only data collected is anonymous counts of which pages are used. Students and parents can't message each other.

📸 Photos and video

Photos wait for the teacher's OK. Dance videos are school Google Drive links shown after approval. The camera mirror never saves or sends video.

©️ Copyright respected

The licensed script, lyrics and tracks enter the app only from the teacher's own copy and stay encrypted for this cast.

🏫 District steps

Technology approval for the app and the Google sign-in setup are being confirmed with the district.
