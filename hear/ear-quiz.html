One at a time:

There are two ways to sort hearing loss. The broad way has 2 types: conductive and sensorineural. The more specific way has 3 types: it splits sensorineural into sensory and neural.

What each part does

Outer ear (external ear)

Auricle / Pinna
The part of the ear on the outside of the head. Sound waves enter here.
Ear canal
The tube that carries sound waves to the tympanic membrane (eardrum).
Tympanic membrane (eardrum)
Divides the outer ear from the middle ear. It vibrates when sound waves hit it and passes the vibrations to the ossicles.
Skull
The bone of the head. The middle ear and inner ear sit inside it.

Middle ear (tympanic cavity)

Ossicles
Three small connected bones: the malleus (hammer), the incus (anvil) and the stapes (stirrup). They amplify the vibrations from the tympanic membrane (eardrum) and send them to the cochlea.
Eustachian tube
Links the middle ear with the back of the nose. It helps equalize the pressure in the middle ear.

Inner ear (labyrinth)

Cochlea
The hearing organ. It is shaped like a snail and filled with fluid. Hair cells inside it turn vibrations into electrical signals.
Stereocilia
Tiny hair-like tips on the hair cells in the cochlea. When they bend, an electrical signal is made. They are too small to see in the picture.
Vestibular apparatus
Holds the receptors for balance.
Vestibulocochlear nerve (cranial nerve VIII)
Carries the electrical signals from the inner ear to the brain.
